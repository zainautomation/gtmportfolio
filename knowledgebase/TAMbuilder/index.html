Build a TAM investors actually believe

Nine steps, each with the questions to answer and the boxes to tick. Built bottom-up: accounts you can count × prices customers really pay.

How to use it

Answer every question in writing. Can't answer one? That's your next research task. Tick a box only when the evidence is written down.

01

Define the problem and buyer

Can't describe the market in one sentence? Then you can't count it.

What specific job does the product do, and for whom?

Who signs the contract, and who uses it daily?

What do buyers use today instead?

One-sentence market definition written
Economic buyer and end user named by job title
Current alternatives listed
02

Segment the market

Segments differ on price and buying motion, so size each one.

Which company sizes feel the problem most?

Which industries and regions can we sell into today?

What trigger says a company needs us now?

3–6 segments defined by size, industry and region
Trigger or tech-stack filters added
No company sits in two segments
03

Choose the method

Lead bottom-up. Use the others only as cross-checks.

Bottom-up · accounts × realistic ACV. Your headline.

Top-down · analyst market × your share. A sanity check.

Value-based · value created × share you charge. New categories.

Bottom-up chosen as the headline figure
One other method lined up as a cross-check
04

Count the accounts

Count companies with the problem, not every company.

Which data source counts each segment best?

Do two sources roughly agree?

Are we counting only companies that have the problem?

Account count recorded per segment
Filters and source saved so anyone can reproduce it
Count cross-checked against a second source
05

Set a realistic ACV

Use what customers actually pay, never list price.

Pricing unit: seat, employee, usage or tier?

What does a typical customer pay after discounts?

Are we including expansion, and is that stated?

ACV set per segment from real deals or competitor pricing
Plan mix and average discount factored in
Expansion assumption stated or excluded
06

Calculate TAM

TAM = Σ (Accounts × ACV), summed across segments.

07

Narrow to SAM and SOM

08

Triangulate

A TAM 10× the category leaders' combined revenue is a red flag.

Is bottom-up within range of top-down?

What do the category leaders earn combined?

Do comps or competitor rounds back the size?

Compared with a top-down source
Gaps over 3× explained or the model fixed
Competitor revenue used as a reality check
09

Document and stress-test

Show your working so the number survives scrutiny.

Which two inputs move the result most?

What if ACV drops 20% or accounts halve?

Who owns the model, and when is it refreshed?

Every assumption listed with its source
Low, base and high cases calculated
Owner and refresh date set

Red flags

Fix any of these before the number goes in a deck.

×Headline is a whole-industry analyst figure
×Counted every company, not those with the problem
×ACV based on list price
×Accounts sit in more than one segment
×TAM and SOM presented as the same number
×Nobody could reproduce the account counts
